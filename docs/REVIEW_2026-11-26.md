# Review, 2026-11-26: the whole game, start to finish

Alex asked for a review of everything up to the end of the game: what makes the loop fun (above all at the start), the economy and whether anything is over- or undervalued, a list of fixes from most to least severe, quality improvements for development and frame rate, and a bigger world to explore. Every name, price and line in this file is a draft.

How it was done: the code and data were read (`data/economy.json`, `wildlife.json`, `requests.json`, `store_orders.json`, `goals.json`, `main.gd`'s knitting and selling paths, `sweater_sale.gd`, `build_plus.gd`, `barista.gd`, `acts.gd`), and the open items in `REVIEW_2026-11-16.md` and `REVIEW_2026-11-25.md` were checked against today's code. Nothing here was play-tested. "Checked" means the code was read and the mechanism confirmed.

---

## 0. The short version

1. **The game can't be finished yet.** Act 1 is complete and Act 2 is mostly there, but Act 3 has three placeholder goals and nothing of its own, and Act 4 has no goals, no God Sweater and no ending. `Game.unlocked()` has real consumers for `dogs` and `town` only; `workers`, `shops`, `mayor` and `god_sweater` open nothing. A player who reaches Act 4 gets an empty goal list.
2. **The economy's biggest fault is that the cheapest animals beat the rare ones per pen place.** A rabbit in the pen earns less than the sheep it displaces, a fox about the same, and a 40-day-respawn chinchilla only 2.5 times a sheep. Rarity pays per sweater but not per pen place, so filling pens with rare animals isn't worth the trips (section 3).
3. **The rarity and quality split Alex decided on 18 November isn't built.** A wild fibre still lifts the knit's grade to its floor (`main.gd` `_finish_knit`: `rarity = maxi(rarity, wildlife.knit_floor(fibre))`), and `fair()` then multiplies by the fibre's value as well. So rare fibre counts twice, and with lynx or sable fluff the rhythm game stops mattering at all (the floor is 5, the top).
4. **Needles do nothing for a decent player.** They raise the lowest grade (steel 2, rosewood 3, silver 4), but anyone who plays the rhythm game reasonably lands a 3 anyway. The 150c and 500c pairs are bought for nothing.
5. **The start is better than it was** (the 25 November stalls are fixed), but the first hour still has no surprise and no sense of a world beyond the farm. This pass adds treasure, hidden huts, two new lands and gated rare animals for exactly that (section 6).
6. **Development is slowed by three things that keep growing:** `AGENTS.md` is now 200 KB (it doubled since 16 November; every agent reads it), `main.gd` is 13,600 lines with the tests inside it, and 2,063 review renders are tracked in a public 4 GB repo.

## 2. Making the loop more engaging, above all at the start

### The first ten minutes

1. **Open on the prettiest picture in the game.** The bus scene is still dim and rainy, seen from far above (`20_act1_rain_bus.png`). Golden hour through the bus window, then the camera settles behind Nora. Players decide in the first 30 seconds.
2. **Something glinting on the way to Gran's.** Put one treasure patch right beside the path from the sign to the farm, with a sparkle, so the very first thing she does with the shovel (once she has one) is dig up a few coins and a note from Gran ("You found my first hiding place. There are more."). That one line turns the whole map into a treasure map. Built in this pass (section 6.1, `gran_first`).
3. **A fox at the edge of the woods while she herds the strays.** It bolts. It's a promise of what the dog is for, and it costs one scripted spawn.
4. **Hints later.** First person, cinematic camera and map hints still show in the first minutes; hold them until after the first sale.

### The first day and the first week

5. **Let the farm produce surprises.** Today a day's rhythm is: shear, knit, walk to town, sell. Add a daily variable: one "today" card in the morning ledger ("A rabbit's been seen by the old well", "Rosa's running low on yarn: double price for a sweater today", "A buried tin's been washed up on the beach"). It's the "small surprises on a timer" from the 24 November review, still unbuilt, and it's the cheapest big win for the middle of Act 1.
6. **The turnips should pay off once.** The first harvest plays a supper scene: energy, happiness, a cozy beat between tasks.
7. **Nora talks to the dog.** One idle line now and then. The dog is the game's mascot and has no personality on screen.

### The mid game

8. **Make exploration pay in more than animals.** Each land should have three things to find: its animals, its treasure patches and its secret. That's what section 6 builds.
9. **Gate the best animals behind an upgrade, not distance alone.** Distance is the only gate today, and the dog runs anywhere. A land you can see but not yet enter (the deep mire, the high dunes) is the strongest pull a game like this has. Built for the swamp in this pass (waders).
10. **Hunts.** A lead (a rumour, a letter, an order) puts a marker in the fog with a time window ("a sand cat in the high dunes, the next three nights"). Legendary and Mythic animals should appear *only* on hunts. This is the 24 November plan's step 6, and it's what Act 2 most needs.
11. **The night should be worth staying up for.** Bonfires and the new tent let her stay out; give her a reason: some animals (lynx, sand cat, the swamp's night heron... drafts) only come out after dark.

### Keeping the knitting game central

12. **Quality from the rhythm game, rarity from the fibre** (Alex, 18 November). Build the split so every knit matters (section 3.2).
13. **Real music.** All four knitting songs still point at `placeholder.ogg` with the same bpm. It's the activity players repeat most.
14. **"Knit the rest quickly"** at her average quality once she's knitted ten, so the rhythm game is chosen for the good sweaters rather than endured.

## 3. The economy: what sells for what, and what's out of balance

### 3.1 What a sweater sells for today

Rosa's price (`market_base` sweater 96c, drifting 80 to 130% each morning, plus 4% a heart) times the grade (`rarity_price` 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.5, 2.4) is the fair price. Face to face multiplies that by the fibre's tier `value` (`wants`: plain 1.0, common 1.2, rare 1.8, ultra rare 2.0, legendary 2.5, mythic 5.0), and the buyer's top price is 0.95 to 1.35 times it.

| Fibre | Grade after its floor (with a 3-star knit) | Face to face (fair) | Rosa | Online store | Request |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sheep's wool | 3 | 96c | 96c | (no orders) | 130 to 210c |
| Angora | 3 | 115c | 96c | 120c | 180c |
| Fox, raccoon | 3 | 173c | 96c | 210c | 250 to 260c |
| Beaver, mink, coyote | 4 | 288c | 144c | 360c | 420 to 450c |
| Sable, lynx | 5 | 576c | 230c | 640c | 800 to 850c |
| Chinchilla | 5 | 1,152c | 230c | 1,400c | 2,000c |

Per sweater, the ladder is good: 1x, 1.2x, 1.8x, 3x, 6x, 12x.

### 3.2 What each place in a pen earns a day

This is where it breaks. A pen place is the real scarce thing (4 a pen, 4 more in the barn), and each wild sweater also uses 2 wool, the same wool that would have made two thirds of a plain sweater. Value per place per day, after taking off the wool it uses:

| Animal | Coat grows back | Per sweater | Per place per day, net of wool |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sheep | 1 day (2 wool) | 96c | **64c** |
| Rabbit | 1 day | 115c | **51c** (worse than the sheep it replaces) |
| Fox | 2 days | 173c | **54c** (no better than a sheep) |
| Mink | 3 days | 288c | **75c** |
| Lynx | 4 days | 576c | **128c** |
| Chinchilla | 7 days | 1,152c | **156c** |

A chinchilla takes ten barks, lives at the far edge of the map and respawns after 40 days, and it's worth 2.4 sheep in the pen. A player who works this out fills the pens with sheep and sells to Rosa.

**Fixes (pick one, then play a day):**

- **Shorter regrow for rare coats** (simplest): fox 1 day, ultra rare 1, legendary 2, mythic 2. Per place per day: fox 109c, mink 224c, lynx 256c, chinchilla 544c. Rare animals then clearly earn their trip.
- **Or more fibre per comb** for rarer animals (a lynx gives 2), which does the same and keeps the coat timer as a visual.
- **And charge the wild sweater less wool** (1 wool + 1 fibre), so a rare animal doesn't also cost a sheep's output.
- **Rosa should pay for fibre too**, at half the face-to-face premium. Today she pays the same for a lynx sweater as for a plain Legendary, which hides the value of rare fibre from anyone who doesn't haggle.

### 3.3 Rarity counted twice, and the rhythm game sidelined

`_finish_knit` sets `rarity = maxi(knit, needles, fibre floor)`, and `fair()` then multiplies by the fibre value on top. Alex's decision (18 November, `DIRECTION.md`): rarity comes from the fibre, quality from the knitting game, both set the price. Build it as two separate numbers on each sweater:

- **Rarity** = the fibre's tier (wool Common ... chinchilla Mythic). Sets the design's stars.
- **Quality** = the knit, 1 to 5 stars, raised only by her playing (needles add a bonus, section 3.4).
- **Price** = Rosa's base × the rarity's value × a quality multiplier (say 0.7, 0.85, 1.0, 1.25, 1.6).

This is the largest change in this review: it touches saves (`Game.sweaters`), every sale path, requests, orders and the goals. It deserves its own session and a full autotest.

### 3.4 Needles

Steel (150c, floor 2) and rosewood (500c, floor 3) do nothing for anyone who knits a 3. Give needles a job a good player also feels:

- each pair widens the rhythm game's hit window (steel +10%, rosewood +20%, silver +30%), or
- adds a flat quality bonus on top of the knit (+0.25, +0.5, +1 star, capped at 5).

The treasure needles added in this pass follow the same ladder, so whatever is chosen they fit (section 6.1).

### 3.5 Coins that don't come from a sweater

| Leak | Size | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The barista: 5c a cup, no daily limit | about 90 to 130c a day of real time at the counter, near a whole Act 1 day's sheep income | Alex chose to keep it (18 November). Fine past Act 1; in the first days it's the best-paying thing in the game. A cap of 40 cups a day would keep it a side game. |
| Furniture built from free felled wood sells back for half its coin price (`build_plus.gd` `sell_value`) | about 2c a wood, so about 16c a tree; trees regrow in 14 days and hundreds stand outside town | **Open.** Sell a wood-built piece back for its wood. |
| Job loot furniture sells for coins | small | Open, the same fix. |
| Treasure coin bags (this pass) | 15 to 120c each, finite, mostly one-offs | New, deliberately petty cash, kept out of the ledger. |

### 3.6 Other prices worth a look

- **Marcus's house at 1,000c** is about a week of early Act 1 income for a three-floor house. It's the best purchase in the game by a mile. Fine as a reward, but say so: it should feel like a lucky deal, not a price.
- **Buying a sheep (400c)** from Rosa when wild sheep are herded in free. Remove it (16 November review, still open) or make Rosa's sheep a breed with better wool.
- **The pastry (20c)** gives 30 energy, 30 food and 40 happiness. It's the best value item in the game; at 35c it would still sell.
- **Gary's two land listings** (Greyspine Hollow 21,000c, Saltmeadow Point 36,000c) still can't be bought.
- **Requests underpay face to face for ultra rare and up** once fibre value applies (mink request 450c against 288c fair: fine; lynx 850 against 576: fine). They're balanced now; recheck after 3.2.
- **The large plot (18,000c)** buys pieces that do nothing (set dressing). Until her town works, it's a trophy.

## 5. Quality improvements: development speed and frame rate

### Development speed

1. **A developer panel** (F10 in the editor build only, like god mode): give coins and items, set the act, the hour and the day, spawn any species next to her, fill the pens, jump to any land. Most agent checks and all of Alex's play-tests start by setting up state by hand; this makes each one seconds instead of minutes.
2. **Split `AGENTS.md`** into a 15 KB index and `docs/agents/<topic>.md` files (Prompt F of 16 November, still open). Agents load only the topic they touch.
3. **Move the checks out of `main.gd`** into `scripts/tests/`, one file per `--only=` group (Prompt E). Twelve groups already live in their own `*_check.gd` files; follow that pattern for the rest.
4. **Untrack the review renders** (`art/**/review/`, `art/**/out/` in `.gitignore`, `git rm -r --cached`). No history rewrite needed.
5. **Snapshot saves for testing.** A `--load=fixtures/act2_day3.json` flag that starts from a saved state, so a check of an Act 2 feature doesn't play Act 1 first.
6. **Fewer quick checks per change.** Most new features now come with their own `--only=` group; keep that, and make the full run a nightly thing rather than per change.

### Frame rate (Medium is about 21 to 34 ms on the M4 Air under load)

| Rank | Fix | Why | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **VRAM-compress the 3D textures.** 1,244 of 1,331 texture imports are still lossless. | Memory and bandwidth; the 2048 px wildlife and character maps are about 22 MB of video memory each. Fewer slow frames. | small to medium |
| 2 | **Decimate the skinned models.** People about 50k triangles, animals 30k, birds 32 to 40k. Aim for 10k, 8k and 2k. | Villagers and wildlife cost 1 to 4 ms; twelve dogs and new lands add more animals. | large (the rig pipelines) |
| 3 | **An LOD for the dogs and wildlife past 25 m**: a quarter of the triangles. | Same, cheaper to do than 2. | medium |
| 4 | **Minimap unshaded** (16 Nov rank 4, still open). | Fewer spikes. | small |
| 5 | **Stream the lands' scenery** as the camera nears, as the grass already does, before the world grows much more. This pass adds two lands. | Draw calls and memory. | medium |
| 6 | **Measure on a quiet machine.** Several perf logs ran under a load of 5 to 20 from other sessions. | Without it every number above is a guess. | process |

## 6. The bigger world (being built in this pass)

Alex asked for secrets, hidden huts, treasure dug with the shovel, a tent from the home goods store, a fuller swamp with gated rare animals, beach huts and crabs, a desert, rolling green hills, animals for each land, and a horse placeholder. What follows is what was built and how it fits the rules.

### 6.1 Treasure and secrets (`scripts/treasure.gd`, `data/treasure.json`)

- **Green patches.** Scattered through every land (and a few round the farm) are small patches of greener, lusher grass with a faint sparkle. With the shovel in hand, a click on one digs it up. What it holds is data: a bag of coins (petty cash, 15 to 120c), a fibre, seeds, a furniture piece for nothing, or one of the rare finds below.
- **Rare finds:** driftwood needles on the beach, bog oak needles in the deep mire, bone needles in the desert, and a few one-of-a-kind items. Each found pair of needles is a step on the same ladder as Rosa's (`needle_floor()`), so a found pair never undercuts silver: the best of them equals rosewood.
- **Most patches are one-offs;** a few "common" ones refill after some days. Coin bags never refill. Found coins are kept out of the ledger.
- **Huts with a chest:** a beach hut, a fisher's stilt hut in the deep mire, a ruined shelter in the high dunes and a shepherd's bothy in the hills. Each holds one chest, opened once.
- **Gran's first hiding place** by the farm path (section 2, point 2).

### 6.2 The tent (`data/furniture.json` "tent", Builder's Delight)

- Bought from Steve's catalogue (Builder's Delight is the `homegoods` shop). Pitched on open ground in any land (the ground ring's "Pitch the tent"), taken down again into her bag.
- **"Sleep in the tent"** sleeps till 6:00 like her bed, and with a bonfire of hers within its warm radius it fills every bar (energy, food, warmth, happiness), not just half a night's energy. Without a fire it rests like the bonfire does. Priced as a real purchase (a draft), since it's better than her own bed out there.

### 6.3 Mirewood Swamp, fuller and gated

- New foliage of its own: cattails, lily pads on the pools, dead snags, toadstools and ferns.
- **The Deep Mire**, the swamp's far west, is behind deep black water. Nora (and the dog with her) can't wade it without **mire waders** from Steve. The swamp's rarest animals live only there.
- New swamp animals: nutria and muskrat (Rare), and the river otter (Legendary) in the Deep Mire only.

### 6.4 Two new lands, east of the meadows

- **The Sunscald Dunes**, a desert: sand dunes, cacti, bleached rocks and dead bushes. Animals: desert hare (Common), fennec fox (Rare), golden jackal (Ultra Rare), sand cat (Legendary, the high dunes only).
- **The Rolling Downs**, broad green hills with lone oaks, stone and wildflowers. Animals: angora goat (Rare, mohair), cashmere goat (Ultra Rare), alpaca (Legendary) and the vicuña (Mythic, on the far tops only). The fibre animals of the real world, which is what a knitting empire should be chasing.
- **The ground is still flat where she walks.** The dunes and hills are raised ridges and mounds that block the way, with walkable valleys and passes between them, the way the mountains are made. True rolling ground that Nora walks up and over is a bigger change (everything that walks, the grass, the click picking and placement assume y = 0) and is its own step.
- The world's walking grid, the road map, the grass and the fog grow east to take them. Painted roads and a save's explored fog are carried over.

### 6.5 Saltmeadow Beach

- Beach huts in a row along the dunes, one of them hiding a chest.
- **Crabs**: small groups that scuttle sideways about the sand and dig in when she comes near, like the birds' flocks. Not catchable.
- The sea otter (Mythic) on the far rocks, gated by being the furthest and shyest animal on the coast.

### 6.6 The horse (placeholder)

- Lars sells a horse in Act 1 once the barn's rent is paid (a draft price). Clicking it, or the `ride` key (R), mounts her; riding goes about two and a half times her running speed. She gets off at doors, buildings, story scenes and first person, and the horse waits where she left it. It never herds and the animals don't count it as a threat.
- **The model is a stand-in:** the dog's rig scaled up and tinted chestnut, until a horse can be made in Tripo. It plugs in through `model` in `data/horse.json`.

### 6.7 Stand-ins to replace when Tripo is back

The new fur animals reuse the existing rigged models, scaled and tinted (the goats and alpaca on the sheep, the fennec and jackal on the fox and coyote, the sand cat on the lynx, the nutria and muskrat on the beaver, the otters on the mink, the hare on the rabbit). Each is one `model` line in `data/wildlife.json` to replace. They are listed under "Models still needed" in `DIRECTION.md`. The crabs, huts, tent, chest and swamp and desert plants are script-built from ChatGPT references, like the café props.
