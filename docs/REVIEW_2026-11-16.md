# Knitting Empire: code and design review (16 November 2026)

> **Update, 18 November:** Alex answered section 5 and the open questions; his decisions are in `docs/DIRECTION.md` ("Rarity, quality and the rest"). Prompt B was mostly done by another session on 17 November (four graphics levels, two shadow splits, the lamp and grass changes). Prompt C's questions are answered there: the barista stays as it is, and rare fibre sets the rarity while the knitting game sets a separate quality.

This is a read-only review. No game code was changed. It covers loose ends, broken or bloated systems, a timeline of the project, the frame rate, the gameplay loop, and ready-to-use prompts for agents who'll do the cleanup.

How sure each claim is:
- **[checked]**: I confirmed it in the code or the logs myself.
- **[reported]**: an audit pass found it with a file and line, and I didn't re-check it.
- **[measured]**: it comes from the `--only=perf` logs in `/tmp/ke_perf*.log`.
- **[unverified]**: a likely cost that nobody has measured yet.

## 0. The short version

1. **The frame rate is limited by the graphics card, not by the scripts.** [measured]
   - The scripts use 1 to 4 ms of each frame, and pausing every script saves about nothing.
   - The real cost is drawing shadows, the lamps' shadow passes, the grass blades and the ground shader.
   - At night in town or in the café, the frame draws 2,500 to 3,500 objects and 3 to 5 million triangles.
2. **Your own saved setting is High.** [checked]
   - `~/Library/Application Support/Godot/app_userdata/Knitting Empire/settings.cfg` says `quality=2`, and High measures 18 to 29 fps on the MacBook Air.
   - Today's change (`GFX_SETTINGS_VERSION`) moves you once to Medium on the next launch. Medium still dips to 30 to 34 ms (about 30 fps) in the café and town at night.
   - So you're probably seeing two things at once: High is too heavy, and Medium is borderline.
3. **The measurements were taken on a busy machine.** [measured]
   - Several perf runs had a load average of 5 to 20, with other Godot or Blender sessions running at the same time.
   - That alone can double the frame time. Some of what you're seeing is other agent sessions using the computer.
4. **The game has outgrown its structure.**
   - `scripts/main.gd` is 11,414 lines, and about 6,700 of them are autotest checks. [checked]
   - `AGENTS.md` is 101 KB, and every agent reads it before starting. [checked]
   - `.git` is 3.5 GB, and about 2,000 review renders are tracked in a repo that's public. [checked]
5. **The loop in `DIRECTION.md` is good, but the game doesn't point the player at it yet.**
   - Act 1 ends, and the first automatic nudge sends her to a bakery job. [reported]
   - There are five ways to sell a sweater, three rarity scales, and two separate animal systems.
   - Several ways of earning coins break "every coin comes from a sweater".

## 3. Broken systems, bloat and structure

### Structure (this is what slows every future change)

- **`main.gd` holds everything** [checked].
  - It's 11,414 lines with 312 functions.
  - About 6,700 of those lines are autotest checks (from about line 3,500 to 9,800, plus about 400 more at the end).
  - Every agent who opens it reads the tests too, and every edit risks a parse error that stops the whole game.
  - **Fix:**
    - move the checks to `scripts/tests/*.gd` (one file per `--only=` group);
    - then pull out the shop and menu rows (`_act`, the villager menus), the house and roof, and the camera into their own scripts.
- **`AGENTS.md` is 101 KB** [checked].
  - That's roughly 25,000 tokens loaded into every agent session before any work starts. It costs money and pushes real context out.
  - **Fix:** a short `AGENTS.md` (the project, how to run it, the test rules, the hard rules, an index of topics) and `docs/agents/<topic>.md` files for the detail (wildlife, the café, build mode, assets and so on).
- **The repo is 3.5 GB** [checked].
  - About 2,000 files under `review/` folders and a 4.3 GB `art/` folder.
  - The repo is public (your choice on 13 November), so those renders are public too.
  - **Fix:**
    - add `art/**/review/` and `art/**/out/` to `.gitignore`;
    - untrack the existing files with `git rm --cached`.
  - Rewriting history to actually shrink `.git` is a separate decision for you: it rewrites every commit.
- **Textures are stored uncompressed** [checked].
  - 1,032 of the 1,299 `.import` files under `assets/` use lossless, non-VRAM compression.
  - The 2048 px wildlife and character maps take about 22 MB of video memory each.
  - Reimport the 3D textures as VRAM Compressed, mark the normal maps as normal maps, and cap wildlife at 1024 px.

### Bugs and faults

- **The perf tool's own faults** [reported, consistent with the logs].
  - After each probe, `perf_check.gd:249` turns the High-only effects back on, so later rows aren't measuring the level they claim.
  - The `vol_fog` probe does nothing, because `main.gd` and `lighting.gd` turn the fog back on every frame. Fog has never actually been measured.
  - The noise between runs is about ±10 ms under load.
- **Penned wild animals have no night behaviour.** Only `sheep_list` goes to bed (`main.gd:9871`) [reported].
- **Sweaters don't record their fibre** (`game_state.gd:284`) [reported]. Per-fibre prices and fibre orders can't be built until this changes.
- **Nothing reacts when an animal is penned.** The event fires (`wildlife.gd:385`), but no scene or goal listens for it. The first catch passes without any reaction.

## 4. The frame rate: recommendations, strongest first

**Target:** Medium holds 30 fps everywhere at 2560 x 1378, and Low holds 60.

High at full resolution isn't realistic on a fanless M4 Air with SDFGI on, so treat High as a setting for desktops.

### Step zero: measure honestly

- Fix `perf_check.gd`: put each probe back to the level's own state, and make `vol_fog` actually switch off.
- Run the baseline with **no other Godot or Blender running**. Several of the logs had load averages of 5 to 20.
- Keep only runs whose calibration rows (Low at the farm) agree within about 2 ms.
- Repeat each toggle twice.

### The fixes

| Rank | Fix | Expected saving | How sure | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cut shadow casters.** Set `cast_shadow` off on candles, books, cups, small café pieces and shop furnishings while the roof is on (`interiors.gd`). Use 2 shadow splits on Low and Medium (`main.gd:572` uses 4 everywhere). Drop the directional shadow map to 2048. | The sun's shadow costs **9 to 14 ms**, and switching it off halves the draws. Cutting the casters should recover a good part of that. | measured (the cost); the saving is an estimate | small |
| 2 | **Lamp shadows.** Change `LAMP_SHADOWS` from `[0, 2, 4]` to `[0, 1, 2]`. Lower `distance_fade_shadow` from 40 to 20 m (`catalog.gd:212`). Switch interior lights off when she's outside and the door is shut. | The lamps cost **15 to 17 ms** at night on High and **6 ms** on Medium, almost all of it in the shadow passes. | measured | small |
| 3 | **Grass blades.** Index the blade mesh (`grass.gd`: no `st.index()`, so 15 vertices where 8 would do) [checked]. Move the patch noise into per-instance data instead of 16 hashes per vertex. Shorten Medium's grass range from 40 to 32 m. | The grass costs **9 to 11 ms** at the farm. | measured (the cost) | small |
| 4 | **Minimap.** Render it unshaded (`debug_draw = UNSHADED`) or cull the props from it. Today it redraws the world and its sun shadows four times a second. | Steadier frames, fewer slow spikes (p95) | unverified | small |
| 5 | **Sky uniforms.** Write them at about 2 Hz instead of every frame, or set the sky to `PROCESS_MODE_INCREMENTAL`. Each change may re-filter the sky's radiance map. | Possibly 1 to 3 ms | unverified | small |
| 6 | **Bake the ground's low-frequency noise into a texture.** It does about 22 noise lookups per pixel today. | The ground costs **4 to 9 ms**. | measured (the cost) | medium |
| 7 | **VRAM-compressed textures** (see section 3). | Memory and bandwidth, and fewer slow frames | unverified | small to medium |
| 8 | **Decimate the skinned models.** Characters are about 50k triangles, animals 30k, and birds 32 to 40k each (37 of them). Aim for 6 to 10k for people and animals and about 1.5k for birds, and cull birds at 25 m. | Villagers and wildlife cost 1 to 4 ms. | measured (the cost) | **large**: these models are generated, so it means rerunning the rig, eyes, grip and light-bake pipelines from sources in Downloads |
| 9 | Smaller items: SSAO quality 3 → 1 at half size on Medium, glow off on Low, the window glass reading the screen with `filter_linear` instead of `filter_linear_mipmap`, rain on `GPUParticles3D`. | about 1 ms each | unverified | small |

Doing fixes 1 to 3 should bring Medium's worst spots from about 33 ms to the low 20s. That needs re-measuring before anyone counts on it.

Script optimisation won't help: the probe runs show the CPU isn't the bottleneck.

## 5. Making the loop more fun and coherent

The loop on paper (find, catch, pen, comb, knit, sell, reinvest) is strong. These changes make the game itself point at it.

1. **After Act 1, point her at the loop.**
   - Replace the "Giorgio asked to see you" nudge with an **Empire goal list**: catch your first wild animal, fill a pen, knit with a wild fibre, fill a request, buy steel needles.
   - Each goal pays a little and introduces the next.
   - Fire a scene the first time an animal is penned. Wren or Rosa saying "you caught an *angora*?" makes the first catch memorable.
2. **Make rarity something she chases.**
   - Put the fibre on every sweater and price by fibre: angora about 1.5x, fox about 2.5x, lynx about 6x.
   - Today rare fibre only matters through requests. That's the risk `DIRECTION.md` already names: "if rare fibre isn't worth much more than wool, nobody leaves town".
3. **Keep the rhythm game deciding the quality** (section 2, item 3). Rare fibre raises the ceiling; skill decides where she lands.
   - Give each song a family of designs (Fair Isle patterns from the Fair Isle song, cables from the cable song), so she can choose what to chase.
   - Get real music for the four songs. The knitting game is the activity players repeat most.
4. **Two collection books.**
   - A **Field Guide** of the ten species: seen, caught, combed, with a picture.
   - A **Lookbook** of the fifty designs: knitted or not.
   - These are the Pokémon pull the direction asks for, and they cost little, since the models and designs exist.
5. **Make each species catch differently.**
   - Today rarity changes the number of barks and how far away an animal lives.
   - Add one behaviour per tier: rabbits zigzag, foxes double back, mink hide in water, lynx only come out at night.
   - Give the dog a stamina bar. That's what the scarves in the direction can upgrade.
6. **Three selling channels, each with its own job.**
   - **Face to face**: the best price, with haggling, one a day per person.
   - **Rosa**: instant and low, for when she needs cash now.
   - **Orders**: mail orders now, the computer's shop later. Requests are the special orders in it.
   - Fold mailbox shipping into Rosa's role, or make it pay a little less.
7. **Give the day a shape.**
   - Morning: comb and shear the pen.
   - Midday: a trip out with the dog.
   - Evening: knit by the fire and post orders.
   - Night: lantern trips for night-only animals.
   - The 20-minute day already supports this, and a soft morning note from Gran's letters could suggest it.
8. **Make every bar matter, or cut it.**
   - Happiness could add a little knitting accuracy, and love could bring requests sooner.
   - If a bar changes nothing, take it off the HUD.
9. **Make the wolves an opt-in risk.** `ROADMAP.md`'s "no punishing fail states" pillar was right for this audience. Options:
   - wolves only take animals from pens far from the house;
   - she always gets the alert in time;
   - stalls make her animals fully safe.
   Then the risk is a choice, as `DIRECTION.md` says.
10. **Remove friction that doesn't serve the loop**: Buy a sheep, Expand House, the unbuyable land listings, and crops on the price board.
