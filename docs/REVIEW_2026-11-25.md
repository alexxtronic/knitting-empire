## 1. The sweaters still show the clothes underneath

Every fit check passes: the knit is on, the skin is tucked, and the size is right. Even so, the pictures (`55_fit_*.png`) show this:

| Who | What shows through |
|---|---|
| Giorgio | His chef's jacket collar and shoulders stand up through the neck. His hands come out through the front of the body, and the knit hangs like a tent. |
| Rosa | Her apron is drawn over the sweater. The sweater is boxy round her middle. |
| Rowan | His shirt collar shows at the neck, and his belt at the hips. |
| Barbara | Her shirt collar shows at the neck, with dark specks near the hem (the satchel). |
| Wren | Her skirt flares out below the hem (fine), but the hem sits on top of it, not over it. |
| Nora, Gary, Sabrina, Steve | Look right. |

Why it happens: `under_sweater` (`build.py`, `tuck_key()`) pulls in only the points that are *under* the knit or within `MARGIN` of it, between the hem and the collar. Whatever stands further out (a stiff collar, an apron's bib, a chef's jacket, a belt buckle) stays where it is and pokes through. The knit's outline also traces the bulky outfit, which makes the tents.

The fix, in the sweater build (one pass per character):

- Tuck harder. Any body point inside the sweater's outline (in XY, between hem and collar), however far out, goes under the knit. Margins only decide how far in.
- Add a per-character list of extra regions to tuck: the collar band up to the jaw (Rowan, Barbara, Giorgio), Rosa's apron bib above the hem, and Giorgio's jacket.
- Fit the outline to the *tucked* body, not the outfit, so the knit stops tenting (Giorgio, Rosa).
- Check from the game's own camera (front, three-quarter, walking), not just the fit render.

## 2. Bugs in the first hour

Most likely to bite first. "Sure" means the code was read and the mechanism confirmed; "likely" means the logic says so but it wasn't played.

1. **Act 1 can become impossible to finish (sure).** The goal says to build the pen "near the strays", and its marker is on them. If the pen closes round a stray, `act1_pen` starts playing at once. In the same frames the herd pens that stray and sends `sheep_penned`. While a scene plays, `story_player.trigger()` ignores the event (`if playing != "": return ""`), and a penned stray leaves the list, so it never sends again. `act1_flock_in` is never set, and every step after it is dead, even after a reload. Fix: queue events that arrive mid-scene, or have the flock step check the pen itself.
2. **Not enough coins for seed and yarn (likely).** Wren's sale is the only money, at 46 to 75c at the opening price, and her range invites a low one. Seed (25c) and yarn (36c) need 61c, and a coffee or a pastry first makes it worse. Fix: Wren pays at least the fair price, or yarn drops to about 30c in Act 1, or Rosa gives the first pack on credit.
3. **Doing a step early wastes the coins (sure).** Buying yarn before planting (the shops are side by side, and Wren names both) fires `yarn_bought` while nothing is listening, so she has to buy it again. Wood before the strays is the same. Fix: let either order count, or keep the flag and check it later. At the very least, Rosa should say "plant those seeds first".
4. **The first sweater sold to anyone but Wren stalls the story (sure).** `first_sale` only comes from Wren's guided sale. Selling to Rosa or someone else leaves "Name your price for Wren" up with no sweater to sell.
5. **Strays can be sheared before they're penned (likely).** The `sheared` event is then lost, and task 3 waits a whole game day for the wool to grow back.
6. **Task 3/4 can come back after it's done (likely).** Knitting Rosa's wool after penning but before shearing finishes the knit step early. Shearing afterwards puts "Rowan 3/4" back on top of 4/4.
7. **Text that's wrong now (sure):**
   - Gran's note in her bag (`homestead.gd` `NOTE_TEXT`) still promises "a hundred coins under the lid" and the seed tin.
   - The seed-tin note says Rosa buys crops.
   - The market toast says "Sold your first sweater" for the second one.
   - Giorgio says "this one's on the house" and gives nothing.
   - The knit toast says "Ship it for Xc", but shipping doesn't count for Rowan's 4/4.
8. The pen builder's F, G and T keys can't be rebound, and its toasts name the keys directly.

## 3. Friction in the opening

- **The first minute is dark.** The bus, the phone and the sign are dim and rainy, seen from far above, with Nora tiny on screen (`20_act1_rain_bus.png`, `21_act1_phone.png`). The first picture a player sees should be the prettiest: bring the camera in close, use a warmer evening light, and keep the rain light.
- **Hints arrive too early.** First person, the cinematic camera and the map hints show in the first minutes, before anything that matters.
- **Dead time.** The turnips take about 6 real minutes, and watering is easy to miss. Nothing in the story uses them.
- **Twelve goals in a row.** From the market through Rowan's four tasks to the porch there's no breather.
- **Steps that fail silently.** Points 3 and 4 above give no message at all.
- **Areas only notice Nora.** Walking the dog to the strays or the porch triggers nothing.
- **No direction to the bleating.** "Find where that bleating is coming from" gives no pointer. A star over Rowan's hill and the bleat as a 3D sound would do it.

## 4. Making the start more fun (small, mostly data)

- **A beautiful first shot.** Golden-hour light on Maple Grove from the bus window, then the camera settles behind Nora. Players decide in the first 30 seconds.
- **Let the player choose the order.** Seed and yarn as one goal, done in either order.
- **Rowan helps if she's stuck.** If she can't herd the strays in after a minute, he whistles them in himself. That also covers bug 1.
- **The turnips pay off.** At the first harvest Nora cooks supper (a short scene, energy and happiness), a cozy beat between tasks.
- **Giorgio's free loaf is real.** It's a pastry in her bag, and it teaches eating.
- **The end of day one shows what she earned.** A morning card: "Yesterday you earned 92c", using the ledger we already have.
- **A surprise in the first hour.** A rare animal glimpsed at the edge of the woods while she's out with the strays: a fox that bolts. It's a promise of what the dog is for.
- **Nora talks to the dog.** One-line barks of personality when she's idle near him ("Who's a good boy. You. Obviously.").

All lines above are drafts.
