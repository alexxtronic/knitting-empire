# Knitting Empire: four acts across one life (review, 24 November 2026)

> **Update, 24 November:** Alex answered section 5: Gary is the third partner, not Max; two animals per dog; ages 25/50/75/100; the skip is the player's choice. His answers are in `docs/DIRECTION.md` ("Decided").

Alex's plan is quoted in `docs/DIRECTION.md` ("Four acts across one life"). This document covers four things: how it fits the game as it stands, what to change in Act 1, how to build Acts 2 to 4 and the romance, and the order to build them in. Every name, price and line here is a draft.

---

## 0. The short version

1. **The plan fits the game.**
   - Nora ages while the town doesn't (the 13 November vision), and the rare thread from the cold mountains is what keeps the town young. That makes the **God Sweater** the payoff the story has been setting up all along. It needs no new lore.
   - The hooks are already in the code: `wolves.tamed()`, the "kids and grannies" note, the "her own city" phase, Gary's plots, the fog over unexplored land, bonfires for nights out, Bretton Oaks' `home_for()` for where people live, the online store, and the stairs and walls tools for building.
2. **Act 1's problem isn't the opening.** It's what comes after the porch scene.
   - The guided part is tight. After it the game goes quiet: nothing says what to aim for next.
   - The fix is the Empire goal list, the Field Guide and the Lookbook. You approved all three on 18 November, but none has been built yet.
3. **Each act should multiply income about tenfold.** It should come from a new kind of lever, not just bigger numbers:
   - Act 1: better fibre and better knitting.
   - Act 2: more animals at once (dogs), and other people's hands (workers).
   - Act 3: owning the places that sell.
   - Act 4: the choice.
4. **Two parts of the plan clash with what's already built.** Section 5 covers both:
   - **Max as a partner.** In the story he's her abusive ex.
   - **Workers' passive income** against "every coin comes from a sweater". This one has a clean fix: the workers' sweaters are sold through a shop.
5. **Build in this order.** First the frame shared by every act (the act number, her age, the time skip, the partner meter). Then Act 2's systems one at a time, then Act 3, then Act 4. Each step should be playable before the next one starts.

## 2. Act 1 (age 25): more fun, a constant sense of progress, cash that scales

### Where it stands (from the numbers in `data/economy.json`)

- **Income.** The two strays give two fleeces a day, which make about two Common sweaters at 70 to 90c. That's about 150c a day before rent (90c a week), food and extra wool.
- **The first purchases.**
  - The steel needles (150c) take about three days.
  - Rosewood needles (500c) take about two weeks.
  - Silver needles (1,500c), Marcus's house (1,000c) and the computer (2,400c) are each a month or more away on plain wool.
- **The step up.** Money only climbs when she starts catching wild animals. Nothing tells her that's the next step except Rowan's advice talks, one a day.
- **What it all adds up to.** Between the porch scene and the first fox sweater there's no goal on screen, and no number that goes up except coins.

### Recommendations

1. **Build the Empire goal list now** (approved on 18 November). Always one to three goals on screen, each paying out when done:
   - catch a wild animal;
   - pen it;
   - comb it;
   - knit with its fibre;
   - sell one face to face;
   - fill a request;
   - buy the steel needles;
   - build a second pen;
   - rent the barn;
   - earn 1,000c in one day.

   Ten to fifteen goals carry Act 1 to its end. The **last goal is the door to Act 2** (see 4.1).
2. **The Field Guide and the Lookbook** (approved on 18 November). They give a collector's reason to go out:
   - The guide shows each species as seen, caught and combed, with a silhouette for the ones not yet found.
   - The Lookbook shows the fifty designs, knitted or not.
   - A completion count on each gives a number that only goes up.
3. **A morning ledger.** When she wakes, one card shows yesterday's takings by channel (face to face, Rosa, the post, the online store) and the change against the day before. This turns cash scaling into something she sees every day, not something she has to work out.
4. **Make each purchase change the next day's number.** Each one should show in the next ledger:
   - Needles lift quality.
   - A second pen adds four animals.
   - The barn keeps the wolves off.
   - The computer opens a fourth channel.

   On the shop shelf, say what each one adds in coins a day ("about +60c a day with your flock").
5. **Rare fibre must be worth far more than wool.** This is the risk already named in `DIRECTION.md`. A fox sweater should sell for about three times a plain one, and a lynx sweater for about ten. Set the multiplier for each fibre in the data, then play a day to check it.

   This is a change to the economy, so it waits for your yes.
6. **Small surprises on a timer.** Every day or two, one of these:
   - a rumour from the townsfolk ("someone saw a mink by the swamp");
   - a rare animal turning up near the farm for one night;
   - a rush of online orders.

   They give a reason to drop what she's doing and go.
7. **Cut the dead time.** A full knit takes one rhythm game per sweater. Once she has knitted ten, offer "knit the rest of the wool quickly" at her average quality. The rhythm game stays for the best results.

   This also sets up Act 2's grannies, who do it for her.
8. **Fix the two leaks from the 16 November review that are still open:**
   - the barista's uncapped 5c a cup;
   - free-wood furniture selling back for coins.

   Both undercut "money is hard to get".

### Act 1's cash targets (drafts to play-test)

| When | Daily takings |
|---|---|
| Day 1 (Wren's sale) | about 50c |
| After the pen (two sheep) | about 150c |
| First wild animals | 300 to 500c |
| End of Act 1 (two or three pens, the barn, steel or rosewood needles, a few rare fibres) | about 1,000c |

## 3. Acts 2 to 4: how to build each one

### 3.1 The time skip between acts

**Recommendation: the player chooses when the skip happens, and only once the act's last goal is done.**

1. The goal list's last goal opens a scene ("Twenty-five years later…"). She can wait as long as she likes before taking it.
2. A short montage card shows what changed:
   - her age;
   - what grew;
   - her partner's new title;
   - anything that happened to Gran (see 5.4).
3. The world carries over. Pens, buildings, animals, money and friendships stay. The calendar jumps, and Nora's model changes.
4. Each act's own systems unlock with it. Act 2 opens hiring, dogs and the town charter. Act 3 opens the election and buying shops. Act 4 opens the choice.

The skip must never come on its own while she's in the middle of something. That's why it's a scene she opens herself.

### 3.2 Act 2 (age 50): hunts, territory, her own town, workers, dogs

**Hunts.**
- A hunt starts from a lead:
  - a townsperson's rumour;
  - an online order for a fibre she's never seen;
  - Rowan's advice.
- The lead puts a marker in the fog and a time window on it ("a lynx in the foothills, the next three nights").
- Out there she follows tracks (footprints decals leading to it), camps by a bonfire (already built), and catches it with the dog pack.
- Legendary and Mythic animals should appear *only* on hunts, so each one is an event.

**Territory.**
- Gary sells plots out in the lands, not just round the farm.
- Owning one clears its fog and lets her build pens there.
- Pens far from home are more at risk from wolves unless a worker or Rowan watches them. That's a reason to hire.

**Her own town.**
- Buying a large plot (the Eastfield Meadows are the obvious place) lets her found a town and give it a name. Every name is a draft.
- She builds houses and shops with the house builder.
- The town has an **appeal score** from what's in it: houses, a café, benches, lamps, roads, gardens.

**Flyers.**
- She prints flyers at the Mayor's Office (a small cost each) and pins them on noticeboards in Maple Grove (a new prop).
- Each day a flyer is up, a townsperson who reads it may move to her town if its appeal is high enough and a house stands empty.
- Townsfolk are already built to live somewhere (`home_spot()`, Bretton Oaks' `home_for()`). A move changes their home.
- People who live in her town can be hired, and they buy sweaters there.

**Workers.**
- **Kids** shear or comb every animal in the pens they're given, each morning, into the pen's store.
- **Grannies** knit that store into sweaters at their own skill. Their quality is a little below hers, and better needles bought for them raise it.
- **How they're paid:** each takes a share of what the sweaters they made sell for. This is the 16 November note's "a percentage of total sales".
- **Where their sweaters go: a shop** (section 5.2).
- **Limits.** Each granny needs a chair in a building she owns. Each kid looks after one pen. The number of seats and pens is the ceiling.

**Dogs.**
- Extra dogs are bought, expensively, from a breeder (Lars, perhaps).
- They follow the dog the player plays and bark when he barks. Their barks add to the fright and reach a little further.
- Each dog adds two to the herd size: 2 dogs herd 4, 3 dogs herd 6, and so on up to 12 dogs herding 24. The only-the-player-herds rule holds, because the pack moves only with the dog she plays.
- Twelve dogs on screen is a performance risk. Past four or so, the extra dogs are drawn as a simpler pack, or only the nearest few are shown.
- The new dog models are for you to make (section 6).

**Act 2's cash target:** about 10,000c a day by its end.

### 3.3 Act 3 (age 75): dozens of workers, mayor, a conglomerate

**Workers at scale.**
- Dozens of workers can't each be a character walking about. That would cost too much frame rate.
- Show a handful in her buildings. Everything else is a **staff ledger**: each building's workers, what they made yesterday, and their pay.

**The large pen.**
- A pen type with no four-animal limit, bought by the size.
- A full pack of dogs fills it in one trip.

**Mayor.**
- Wren is the mayor now. Two ways in, and offer both:
  - an **election**: popularity from friendships, donations to the town and the shops she owns;
  - a **buy-out**: Wren is tired of the job and will step down for a sum.
- Wren stays as her deputy, so nobody is lost.
- Mayor powers: a small tax on the town's shops, and building in the town square.

**The conglomerate.**
- She buys Maple Grove's shops one at a time: the Yarn Barn, the bakery, the café, Builder's Delight.
- Each pays a daily cut, and the keeper stays on.
- To keep "sweaters are the economy", each shop she owns also sells her sweaters.

**Act 3's cash target:** the 16 November ceiling, about 100,000c a day.

### 3.4 Act 4 (age 100): the choice

- **The God Sweater.** The "ultra rare wool" comes from one animal in the cold mountains, above the Mythic chinchilla. A warm enough sweater is needed to get there, which is the 15 November cold-land idea.
  - Paying for it costs **90% of her coins**, not her assets.
  - She knits the God Sweater in a special rhythm game.
  - She becomes 25 again, using the Act 1 model, and keeps her town and her income.
  - The game carries on with no end.
- **Playing on.** She keeps her money and her age.
  - A gentle decline: energy and walking speed fall slowly.
  - At some point an ending scene plays: her town, her partner, what she built, a last line.
  - The save stays, to look at but not to play.
- Make the choice a scene with her partner and Gran's letters. It's the emotional end of the game.

## 4. The romance: one partner, three acts of promotion

- **The meter.** Each candidate's `romance` stat is already in the game. The candidate with the highest one, above a threshold, can be asked out. Asking makes him her **partner** (`Game.flags["partner"]`).
- **Titles by act.**
  - Act 1: boyfriend.
  - Act 2: fiancé, with a proposal scene at the time skip if he's still the one.
  - Act 3: husband, with a wedding at the town square.
  - Act 4: still her husband, in the choice scene.
- **Switching.** If another candidate's romance climbs past the partner's by a margin, a scene asks her to choose.
  - Staying lowers the other man's romance a little.
  - Switching gives the new man the partner's title for that act and leaves the old one sore.
- **Drama.** For several days after a switch, or while two are close, the losing man is **sore**. Sore men:
  - argue with each other in town (a short scene);
  - say something sharp to her;
  - pull **mild, silly sabotage**, one prank a few days apart:
    - letting one animal out of the pen (she has to herd it back);
    - hiding her needles for a day (her knit is one grade lower);
    - telling one customer her sweaters itch (that person won't buy today);
    - eating the pastries in her bag;
    - painting a wall of her house a garish colour (the paint tool already exists).

  Nothing is lost for good, and a gift or a talk ends it.
- **The ageless town.** Her partner doesn't age, because he's one of the town. That makes Act 4's choice personal: stay old beside him, or wear the sweater and join him.

## 5. Where the plan clashes with what's built (questions for you)

### 5.1 Max as a partner

In `data/scenes/max.scene` and `docs/CHARACTERS.md`, Max is Nora's **abusive ex-husband**:
- She left him.
- He comes to the farm to win her back and shows his temper.
- She can have him arrested.
- AGENTS.md: "anything new that would bring Max back must check `max_arrested`".

Making him a partner who goes from boyfriend to husband would tell players that going back to an abuser is a romance route. For a cozy game about rebuilding a life, I don't think that's what you want.

**My recommendation:** keep Max as he is, the shadow she gets free of, and give the third partner slot to someone else. Candidates:
- **Steve** at Builder's Delight;
- **Gary** at Willow Homes;
- someone new in Act 2's town.

The other option is a redemption arc, where he changes over decades of therapy. It would need very careful writing, and it would undo the arrest ending.

### 5.2 Workers' passive income and "every coin comes from a sweater"

There's no clash if the grannies' sweaters are sold through **her own shop**. The downtown shop from `DIRECTION.md` Phase 3 sells a few sweaters a day on its own and reports in the morning ledger.

So the income is passive and still comes from sweaters, while selling face to face stays the best-paying channel.

### 5.3 How many animals one dog herds

Today one dog herds **3** (`max_herd` in `data/wildlife.json`). Your example says "the usual 2", with 2 per dog after that. There are two ways to do it:

- **Two per dog.** One dog drops from 3 to 2. That makes Act 1 slower: fewer animals per trip.
- **3 + 2 for every dog after the first.** That gives 3, 5, 7 … 25. Act 1 stays as it is.

My pick is the second, since you asked to keep the economy as it is.

### 5.4 Ages and Gran

`docs/CHARACTERS.md` has Nora "in her thirties". Act 1 says she didn't knit for six years after the navy sweater. At 25, that marriage began at about 18.

Either Act 1 changes to 25 (small text edits), or the acts become 30, 55, 80 and 105.

Gran is alive in Florida in Act 1. By Act 2 she'd be well over a hundred. A gentle answer:
- her letters stop in Act 2;
- the farm becomes Nora's (the rent ends);
- one letter, left for her, comes in the post.

### 5.5 What only you can supply

- Nora at 50, 75 and 100, as Tripo models in the same style. Each one is rigged like Nora, with real eyes, the rigid head, her fitted sweater and her tool grip.
- A granny and a kid worker model. Use the townsfolk models, or make two new ones.
- Extra dogs: a few breeds, or colours of the retriever.
- The God Sweater's look, and the ultra rare animal from the cold mountains.
